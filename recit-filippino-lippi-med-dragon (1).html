A Dragon in the Heart of a Pagan Temple

A Dragon in the Heart of a Pagan Temple

Saint Philip and the Dragon — Filippino Lippi

In the ancient city of Hierapolis, the inhabitants worship the god Mars. But one day, a dragon emerges from the temple and spreads a poisonous breath. Several people collapse, including the son of the priest responsible for the cult.

Saint Philip intervenes. Facing the monster, he brandishes no weapon: in the name of Christ, he orders it to leave the temple and disappear into the desert.

The dragon flees. Philip then prays for the victims, who return to life.

But why did Filippino Lippi paint a dragon amid ancient architecture?

Because this creature represents much more than a monster. In the legend, it embodies the evil forces associated with the old idols. By driving it away, Saint Philip demonstrates the victory of the Christian faith over paganism.

Filippino Lippi gives the episode a spectacular dimension: a temple with strange decorations, distressed figures, and an almost fantastical dragon. The scene resembles a theatrical performance witnessed by the viewer.

This story comes from the legendary tradition of Saint Philip, notably transmitted through the Acts of Philip and the Golden Legend. It is not a historically established event.
