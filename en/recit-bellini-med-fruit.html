A pear… or an apple?

A Pear, an Apple… or a Symbol?

A fruit resting on the stone parapet seems insignificant. Yet in Christian painting, even the smallest detail can open the door to several interpretations.

Mary, a New Eve?

In Christian tradition, Mary is sometimes described as the New Eve. Where sin entered the world through Eve's disobedience, Christ brings the promise of salvation.

The fruit might therefore suggest the redemption brought by Christ. This interpretation is associated with the Alzano Madonna in a catalogue entry published by the National Gallery of Australia.

The "Fruit of Thy Womb"?

Art historian Charles Hope has proposed another interpretation for certain Madonnas painted by Bellini.

The fruit might echo the words of the Hail Mary: "Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus."

This reading connects the painted fruit with the Incarnation of Christ. Hope nevertheless acknowledges that the hypothesis cannot be established with certainty.

What If It Isn't a Pear?

Italian agronomist Isabella Dalla Ragione, a specialist in historical fruit varieties, has suggested that a fruit depicted in one of Bellini's Madonnas might be an old apple variety called muso di bue, whose shape can resemble that of a pear.

However, this identification does not necessarily concern the Alzano Madonna. It would therefore be misleading to apply it directly to the fruit in our painting.

Dare to Look

A pear, an apple, a religious symbol? These interpretations do not all answer the same question.

Identifying the fruit, understanding its meaning and establishing the painter's intention are three different tasks.

An almost unnoticed detail reminds us of an essential lesson: looking at a work of art also means accepting that we cannot know everything.
