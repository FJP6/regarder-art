A pear… or an apple?

On the red marble ledge, just to the right of Bellini’s signature, lies a small fruit. Its elongated shape has led viewers to identify it as a pear, and the Alzano Madonna is also known as the Madonna della Pera. The agronomist Isabella Dalla Ragione has suggested that it is an old variety of apple, the muso di bue, whose shape can resemble a pear. Both identifications therefore concern the same fruit painted by Bellini.

Why does Bellini give it this place, just below the Child? The National Gallery of Australia reads it as an allusion to Mary as the New Eve. On this reading, the fruit recalls the Fall. In Christian tradition, the Child Mary presents is the one through whom redemption comes. A single detail thus brings together two distant episodes in the biblical story.

The art historian Charles Hope offers another reading for the fruit that appears in several of Bellini’s Madonnas. It may evoke the words of the Hail Mary, in which Jesus is called “the fruit of thy womb”. Look back at the painting. The fruit rests on the stone, and the Child rests on his mother’s lap. Bellini places them close enough for a familiar object to call to mind the words of a prayer.
