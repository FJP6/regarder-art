Saint Sebastian: why all those arrows?

A body pierced by arrows, yet often a serene face: why did Renaissance painters depict Saint Sebastian so often?

A condemned soldier

According to Christian tradition, Sebastian was a Roman soldier who refused to renounce his faith under Emperor Diocletian. Archers shot him with arrows and left him for dead. But he survived: Irene took him in and tended his wounds.

He later confronted the persecution again and was put to death. The arrows help us identify him, but they do not tell his whole story.

Why did painters return to him?

He was invoked as a protector against the plague. For artists, his nearly naked body also offered an opportunity to study anatomy, proportions and the beauty of ancient sculpture.

The same subject could become an image of suffering, resilience or ideal beauty. The setting, gestures and expressions change from one painter to another.

Look closely

When you encounter Saint Sebastian, examine the body, face and setting. What is each painter telling us beyond the arrows?

The arrows tell us about his martyrdom; the painter’s gaze gives meaning to his body.
