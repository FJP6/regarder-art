Thomas: Believing Without Seeing?

Gospel of John (20:24–29)

After Jesus' resurrection, the disciples tell Thomas that they have seen Christ alive. But Thomas refuses to believe them: he wants to see the nail marks and touch the wound left by the spear.

Eight days later, Jesus appears again. This time, Thomas is present. Christ invites him to examine his wounds and reach towards his side.

Standing before him, Thomas acknowledges his Lord. Jesus then tells him: “Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed.”

A detail to look at

Verrocchio chooses the moment when doubt meets evidence. Christ opens his garment as Thomas reaches towards him. This simple gesture tells the entire story.

Key takeaway: the sculpture depicts the passage from doubt to faith, but also a deeply human moment: the desire to understand things for oneself.
