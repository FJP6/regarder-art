Sfumato: when outlines dissolve

Where does the face end?

Look at the transition between the cheek and shadow, then at the sides of the nose. Can you follow a sharp line everywhere? With sfumato, some boundaries become difficult to pinpoint: tones blend gradually into one another.

Painting like smoke

The Italian word evokes smoke. It describes soft transitions through which a form seems to emerge from darkness. Volume is built through subtle shades rather than a strong outline.

Figures enveloped in shadow

In The Virgin of the Rocks, notice how the faces belong to the surrounding half-light. The figures seem to share the same air as the grotto. Leonardo connects painting with his investigations into the perception of light and form.

Take a closer look

Look first at the hands, then at the cheeks. Find the precisely defined areas and those where the form merges into shadow.
