Five ways to look

Open a theme. These perspectives overlap: one artist may illuminate several questions. Faith, light and colour run through the entire journey.

01Bringing figures to lifeGestures, emotions and stories: a new sense of presence.+

From Giotto to Titian, discover how artists bring figures to life through expressions, gestures, emotions and movement.

Giving emotion a facePain, tenderness and surprise become visible in faces.Letting gestures speakHands and postures tell what words leave unsaid.Telling a story in a single momentA single decisive gesture can convey an entire event.Revealing an inner lifeA portrait reveals a thought, a character or a mystery.Setting bodies in motionBodies come alive and lead the viewer’s eye.Bringing an entire scene to lifeMany figures come together to create a living narrative.

02Art and sciencePerspective, geometry, light and observation.+

During the Renaissance, artists learned to measure, invent and observe. Drawing became a tool for knowledge without losing sight of emotion.

Measure — Geometry creates spaceThe painter does more than depict: they construct a space the eye can travel through.Invent — Drawing becomes a machineDrawing helps artists imagine mechanisms, reveal their workings and understand how they function.Understand — The human body between science and beautyStudying the body, its muscles and proportions transforms the representation of human beings.Observe — Nature under the magnifying glassAn animal, a plant or a feather becomes worthy of careful observation.Feel — Painting the invisibleBeyond accurate forms, artists seek to make emotions and thoughts visible.

03Exploring the body and beautyAnatomy, movement, ideals of beauty and sensuality.+

From the expression of emotion to the sensuality of Venus, discover how the body changed in Renaissance art through six ways of looking.

The body rediscovers emotionFrom anguished faces to physical presence: the body now expresses what a human being feels.Daring to show the nude bodyThe nude is no longer simply a classical reference: it becomes vulnerable, alive and sometimes unsettling.Setting the body in motionThe body twists, struggles, runs and rises. Artists explore its every possibility.Inventing an ideal of beautyProportion, harmony and mythology: how can ideal beauty be represented?When beauty becomes desireNudity changes meaning with the gaze, setting and gestures: from an imagined goddess to a woman looking back at us.The body becomes superhumanAnatomy becomes monumental, expressive, almost impossible: the body exceeds human scale.

04The human being at the centre of the imagePortraits, equestrian monuments, famous figures, tombs and heroes.+

A second way to explore Renaissance art: follow the changing place of the individual in paintings and sculpture, rather than one artist at a time.

Finding a place beside the sacredFrom kneeling patrons to human figures shown at the scale of saints.Existing through one’s faceThe portrait reveals identity and individuality.Entering historyThe equestrian monument celebrates personal achievement.Becoming illustriousWarriors and writers become civic models.Defying oblivionFunerary monuments preserve individual memory.Saints and heroesSaints and heroes acquire a deeply human presence.

05Reinventing AntiquityArchitecture, sculpture and mythology: drawing on the past to invent something new.+

Renaissance artists rediscover the forms and myths of Antiquity. Rather than simply copying them, they transform them to create new approaches to architecture, sculpture and painting.

Rediscovering the language of the ancientsColumns, arches and proportions: ancient forms become a new language.Reviving ancient heroesThe body, the equestrian monument and the power of myth.The return of the ancient godsVenus and Galatea reappear in a poetic world.Inventing ancient illusionPainting architecture and spaces that seem real.Bringing Antiquity to lifeMovement, desire and drama: myths become living scenes.
