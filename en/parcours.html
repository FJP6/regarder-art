Five ways to look

Open a theme. These perspectives overlap: one artist may illuminate several questions. Faith, light and colour run through the entire journey.

01Bringing figures to lifeGestures, emotions and stories: a new sense of presence.+

Faces, gestures and movement give figures a tangible presence. Religious stories also become human experiences: tenderness, pain, surprise and relationships between people.

Piero della Francesca → In the Baptism, the familiar gestures of the angels and the man undressing bring the sacred closer to everyday life.

Look for yourself

Look at the faces close together and the hands: how do they make tenderness visible?

02Constructing spacePerspective, light and colour: organising and transforming how we see.+

Les artistes construisent la profondeur par les lignes, les proportions, la lumière et les couleurs. Ils peuvent aussi jouer avec le point de vue : le soubassement et le cavalier du monument peint par Uccello suivent deux constructions de perspective différentes.

Piero della Francesca → In Piero’s work, distances and architecture connect scenes whose relationship remains enigmatic.

Look for yourself

Follow the lines of the vault: where is the viewer positioned?

Et avec Uccello ? The Hawkwood monument combines two perspective systems: another way of playing with the viewer’s gaze.

03Exploring the body and beautyAnatomy, movement, ideals of beauty and sensuality.+

The body becomes a field of exploration and expression. Anatomy, posture, drapery and nudity can convey strength, vulnerability or an ideal of beauty. Each artist offers a different vision.

Look for yourself

Find the supporting leg, the movement of the hips, and the tension or relaxation of the body.

04The human being at the centre of the imagePortraits, equestrian monuments, famous figures, tombs and heroes.+

A second way to explore Renaissance art: follow the changing place of the individual in paintings and sculpture, rather than one artist at a time.

05Reinventing AntiquityArchitecture, sculpture and mythology: drawing on the past to invent something new.+

Artists study ancient buildings, sculptures and stories. Rather than simply copying them, they reinterpret these sources to create new churches, figures and scenes.

Avec Uccello, the painted equestrian monument adopts the language of commemorative sculpture.

Piero della Francesca → The Sacred Conversation places its figures within architecture inspired by Antiquity.

Look for yourself

Identify the forms inherited from Antiquity and how they are arranged anew.
