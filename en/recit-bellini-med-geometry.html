A family portrait?

Bellini and Mantegna, Familiar Faces?

In 1453, Andrea Mantegna married Nicolosia Bellini, daughter of the painter Jacopo and sister of Gentile and Giovanni. The marriage brought together a young painter who had studied ancient art in Padua and a Venetian family of painters.

In Bellini’s Painting

Look at the faces around Mary and Simeon. On the left, some have suggested that the two women are Nicolosia and her mother, Anna. In the centre, Joseph may have the features of Jacopo Bellini, Giovanni’s father. One of the men on the right may be Giovanni himself. Two names have been suggested for the other, his brother-in-law Andrea Mantegna and his brother Gentile.

In Mantegna’s Painting

Look again at Mantegna’s Presentation at the Temple. The faces at either edge of the painting have also drawn attention. The woman on the left may be Nicolosia, and the man on the right Mantegna himself.

These identifications remain hypotheses. As you move from one painting to the other, notice where the two painters place these faces. They stand at the edges of the scene, close enough to witness the meeting between Mary and Simeon.

In 1460, Mantegna settled in Mantua in the service of the Gonzaga family. Giovanni continued his career in Venice, where he received commissions for religious paintings. Their works continued to circulate between these two circles.
