Bringing the Viewer into the Painting

Mantegna pushes perspective until it becomes a source of surprise: a ceiling seems to open, a body advances towards us, and a painted figure appears as solid as a statue.

He invented neither perspective nor foreshortening. His boldness lies in using them with a new intensity to act upon our perception. The viewer’s position becomes an essential part of the work.
