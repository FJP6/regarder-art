Filippino Lippi

A pupil of Botticelli and heir to Masaccio, Filippino Lippi brings unease and imagination into the Florentine Renaissance

In a few wordsThe works

In a few words

Born around 1457, Filippino Lippi was the son of the painter Fra Filippo Lippi. Trained in Botticelli's workshop, he helped complete the frescoes of the Brancacci Chapel begun by Masaccio. Yet he soon developed his own style: expressive figures, richly detailed settings and unusual architecture. By the late fifteenth century, his paintings revealed a less serene, more restless and theatrical Renaissance.

How to recognize Filippino Lippi

1. Delicate, expressive faces

His figures have intense gazes, refined features and subtle gestures. They seem filled with inner emotion.

2. Rich, bustling compositions

Filippino loves filling his paintings with figures, movement and small secondary scenes. The viewer’s eye is constantly drawn from one detail to another.

3. Almost fantastical classical settings

Columns, statues, elaborate decorations and imaginary architecture fill his scenes. Antiquity becomes a spectacular, sometimes unsettling backdrop.

4. A dramatic, restless Renaissance

While other artists seek balance and serenity, Filippino favours dynamic poses, swirling drapery and scenes charged with tension.
