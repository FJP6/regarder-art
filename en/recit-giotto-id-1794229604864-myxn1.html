Why does Judas kiss Jesus to have him arrested?

Judas is one of the twelve apostles, Jesus’s closest companions. According to the Gospel of Matthew, he agrees to hand Jesus over to the chief priests for thirty pieces of silver.

After his last meal with his disciples, Jesus goes to Gethsemane, near Jerusalem, to pray. Judas arrives with armed men. He has arranged a sign to show them whom to arrest: a kiss.

This gesture of closeness becomes the signal for the arrest. Judas approaches Jesus and kisses him. Jesus is seized and led away: the story of his Passion continues through his condemnation and crucifixion.

Looking at Giotto’s fresco
At the centre of the fresco, Judas’s yellow cloak almost completely envelops Jesus. Their faces are close, their eyes meet. Around them, torches, weapons and gestures create a restless crowd.

Giotto concentrates all the tension in this encounter: the man who has come to betray Jesus is also the man approaching him with a gesture of affection.

A kiss brings bodies closer. Here, it breaks trust.
