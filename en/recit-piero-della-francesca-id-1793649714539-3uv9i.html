Architecture that leads our gaze towards Mary

Straight lines, curves and a central axis: Piero uses geometry to organise how we look.

In this Sacred Conversation, also known as the Montefeltro Altarpiece, the figures occupy a carefully constructed architectural space. Follow the annotations to discover how perspective and composition work together.

1 · The arch — yellow
The great arch frames the coffered vault and crowns the group. Its nearly symmetrical shape reinforces the balance of the architecture around Mary.

2 · The central axis — green
The vertical line passes through the centre of the shell, the suspended egg and Mary’s figure. This alignment connects the architectural setting with the sacred group and emphasises the Virgin’s central position.

3 · Echoing curves — purple
Above, one curve highlights the shell’s lower edge. Below, another suggests the enclosing arrangement of the figures around Mary. Opening towards one another, these curves help create the impression of a space that shelters and gathers the group.

The lower curve is a visual guide: it does not follow a physical outline in the painting or imply that every figure stands on a perfect semicircle.

P · The vanishing point — red
The red lines follow selected edges of the side cornices, then extend towards Mary’s face. Point P approximately marks their convergence. The blue line represents the horizon line, at the viewer’s eye level within the painted space.

Here, perspective directs our gaze towards the central figure: Mary is both the heart of the group and the focus of the architectural construction.

Takeaway
The lines lead towards Mary, the central axis emphasises her presence, and the curves embrace the group. Piero’s geometry contributes to the scene’s unity and stillness.

The annotations are approximate educational guides. Dashed lines extend visible edges; purple curves highlight compositional relationships.
