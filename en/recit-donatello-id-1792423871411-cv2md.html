The Most Vulnerable Is Not the Weakest

In the biblical story, Goliath is a fearsome Philistine warrior who challenges the Israelites. No soldier dares to face him. David, a young shepherd, volunteers.

He is given a suit of armour, but takes it off because it restricts his movements. He steps forward with his sling and a few stones, convinced that God will help him.

A stone strikes Goliath on the forehead, bringing him down. David then seizes the giant’s sword and cuts off his head. The young man whom no one had taken seriously has won the battle.

What to look for in the sculpture:

Donatello chooses to depict the moment just after the victory. David stands with one foot resting on Goliath’s head. The enormous sword recalls the strength of his opponent, while his youthful body emphasises the contrast.

But why is he naked, apart from his hat and boots? Donatello is not trying to illustrate the biblical story literally: he reinvents the hero through the nude figure, reconnecting with the art of Antiquity.

Here, victory does not take the form of a giant. It takes the shape of an adolescent, both vulnerable and self-assured.
