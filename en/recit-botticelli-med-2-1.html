Aphrodite or Venus: a goddess born from the sea

To the Greeks she is Aphrodite ; to the Romans, Venus . The goddess of love, beauty and desire holds power over gods and humans alike.
There are several accounts of her birth. In the Greek poet Hesiod’s version, Cronus mutilates his father Uranus, the Sky, and throws his genitals into the sea. Aphrodite emerges from the foam: beauty born from an act of violence. Botticelli leaves that violence outside the picture. The goddess is already born, standing on a shell and carried towards the shore by the winds. Zephyr, the west wind , blows from the left, accompanied by a female figure sometimes identified as Aura, the breeze.

A young woman, often recognised as a Hora of spring, awaits her with a cloak. Behind this peaceful image lies a less innocent story: desire emerging from conflict among the gods.

**Hesiod · Theogony, lines 188–206 ↗**
