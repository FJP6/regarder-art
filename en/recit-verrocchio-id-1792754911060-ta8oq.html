The Angel Who Supposedly Made Verrocchio Give Up Painting

The Angel Who Supposedly Made Verrocchio Give Up Painting
Look at the two kneeling angels on the left of The Baptism of Christ. They appear to belong to the same scene. Yet one of them hides a remarkable story.

According to Giorgio Vasari, Verrocchio entrusted a young apprentice with painting one of the angels. When the master saw the result, he was supposedly so impressed by its quality that he decided never to pick up a paintbrush again!

But who was this apprentice?

It was Leonardo da Vinci, then training in Verrocchio's Florentine workshop.

The angel on the left, generally attributed to Leonardo, stands out for the softness of its face, the flowing hair and the subtle transitions between light and shadow. The young painter is also thought to have contributed to the landscape.

The truth behind the legend

The story comes from Vasari's Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects, published in the sixteenth century, decades after the events. There is no proof that Verrocchio actually gave up painting out of frustration.

What makes the work remarkable, however, is that it offers a glimpse of the beginnings of an artist who would become one of the most celebrated figures of the Renaissance.

Key takeaway: behind an unassuming angel stands a young Leonardo da Vinci. And behind an entertaining anecdote lies a fascinating question: how does a pupil develop a style of his own?
