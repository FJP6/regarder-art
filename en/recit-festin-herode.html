What is happening before our eyes?

The Feast of Herod — A dance, a promise, a tragedy

Why does a head appear at a banquet?

John the Baptist, the very man who baptised Jesus, has condemned the union of Herod Antipas and Herodias, his brother’s wife. Herodias resents him and wants him dead.

At Herod’s birthday banquet, Herodias’s daughter — known as Salome — dances before the guests. Delighted, Herod promises to grant her whatever she asks. At her mother’s prompting, she demands John the Baptist’s head on a platter.

Herod is distressed. But, before his guests, he refuses to go back on his word. John is executed: the celebration becomes the scene of a murder.

Look at the relief

On the right, Salome dances. On the left, a kneeling servant presents John the Baptist’s head at the banquet table. Donatello brings two successive moments together within a single architectural setting: our gaze moves from the celebration to its consequences.

The arcades and staircases do more than create depth: they also give the story a space in which to unfold.

Biblical sources : Mark 6, 17–29 ; Matthew 14, 3–12.
Reading the relief : Palais des Beaux-Arts de Lille collection record.
