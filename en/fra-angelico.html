Fra Angelico

He inherited Fra Angelico’s luminous colours, elegance and love of detail, but his world became more animated and lavish.

From the splendour of paradise to the silence of a cloister.
In a few words

A Florentine painter and Dominican friar, Fra Angelico (c. 1395–1455) combined Gothic delicacy with Renaissance innovations. His light, colours and restrained gestures bring a human presence to sacred stories.

4 works to understand him

Look before explaining

The Coronation of the Virgin

Does paradise need perspective?

1. Marble steps — The marble steps guide the eye towards the coronation and create a new sense of depth.

2. Saints and angels — A multitude of faces and gestures gathers around the central scene.

3. Gold and colour — Gothic splendour remains, while drapery and bodies acquire volume.

For Fra Angelico, paradise remains wondrous, but it begins to acquire depth.

The Last Judgment

Paradise on the left, hell on the right. Which side would you choose?

1. The dance of the blessed — Angels and the blessed hold hands: happiness becomes a dance.

2. The turmoil of hell — Bodies and gestures writhe, in stark contrast with the harmony of paradise.

3. The open tombs — The open tombs lead the eye into depth and separate the two destinies.

For Fra Angelico, paradise dances while hell struggles.

The Deposition from the Cross

How can sorrow be painted without despair?

1. Gestures — The figures support Christ tenderly: emotion is expressed with restraint.

2. Colours — Pinks, blues and reds bring unexpected radiance to this scene of mourning.

3. The landscape — Hills, trees and buildings open up a space for the eye to explore.

For Fra Angelico, even sorrow retains its gentleness.

The Annunciation at San Marco

What if silence could be painted?

1. Mirrored gestures — The angel and Mary bow their heads and fold their arms: a wordless dialogue.

2. Peaceful architecture — Columns, arches and floor lines structure the space without spectacle.

3. Soft light — Diffuse light replaces golden splendour and invites contemplation.

Fra Angelico no longer needs gold to bring the divine into painting.

Benozzo Gozzoli

Four clues to recognise his distinctive vision.

Radiant colours

Gold, delicate pinks and deep blues give the scenes an almost heavenly glow.

Gentle faces

Downcast eyes and restrained expressions convey serenity.

A new sense of space

Perspective, landscapes and architecture meet Gothic elegance.

Spirituality without commotion

A gesture or soft light can be enough to suggest the sacred.
