A Body Receding into Depth

We stand near the feet, slightly above the body. Instead of showing Christ in profile, Mantegna arranges his body in the depth of the picture.

1 — The feet in the foreground. The blue line locates them: we are very close to the body. It is not an anatomical measurement.

2 — The knees, further away. The coral line marks another level. Notice the short apparent distance between the feet and knees: long legs occupy little height in the image. This is foreshortening.

3 — The face at the far end. The gold outline draws attention to the head. The dotted axis simply guides the eye from feet to face; it is not a calculated vanishing line.

Perspective made readable. Mantegna does not mechanically follow a camera: he adjusts the relationships between body parts so that the feet, wounds and face remain legible. The Pinacoteca di Brera highlights these adjustments.

Try it: hide the guides. Does your eye travel from the feet towards the face? Foreshortening does more than display skill: it brings us close to the dead man and the grief of those beside him.
