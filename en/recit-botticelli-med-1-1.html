Three generations of the Medici around the Child

Look at the old man kneeling before the Child. Vasari recognised him as Cosimo the Elder. The other two Magi may be his sons, Piero de’ Medici, in a red cloak in the foreground, and Giovanni de’ Medici, dressed in white to his right. All three had died by the time Botticelli painted the panel.

Two of Cosimo’s grandsons may also be among the onlookers. Look for Lorenzo the Magnificent, shown in profile in black and red on the right, and Giuliano de’ Medici, the young man with a sword in the left foreground.

If these identifications are correct, three generations of the Medici are gathered around the Child. By commissioning the painting for his chapel in Santa Maria Novella, the Florentine Guasparre del Lama could pay tribute to a family whose wealth and alliances gave it considerable influence in the Florentine republic.

Look again at Cosimo. As he bows before the Child, he makes the humblest gesture in the scene, in its most prominent place.
