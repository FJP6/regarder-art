Fra Filippo Lippi

A painter-monk who gave the figures of paradise the faces of everyday life.

In a few words

Fra Filippo Lippi (c. 1406–1469) grew up in Florence and joined the Carmelite order. Both a painter and a friar, he observed the faces, gestures and feelings of those around him. His Madonnas have the tenderness of real women; his angels, the spontaneity of children. Among his pupils was Botticelli, and his son Filippino Lippi also became a painter.

How to recognise Fra Filippo Lippi

Soft, familiar faces; Madonnas who resemble young Florentine women; angels with childlike expressions; and gestures full of tenderness. In Fra Filippo Lippi’s work, sacred figures feel profoundly human.

How did a Carmelite friar become a father?

According to the traditional account, Filippo Lippi fell in love with Lucrezia Buti, a novice in Prato, who became the mother of his son Filippino. The story shaped his reputation as an unruly painter; much of its detail comes from later accounts, notably Vasari.

Why does a severed head appear at the banquet?

Salome dances before Herod. At the urging of her mother Herodias, she asks for the head of John the Baptist. Lippi brings several episodes of this tragic story together in one fresco (Matthew 14:1–12; Mark 6:17–29).
