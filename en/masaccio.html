Masaccio

A new realism in painting: bodies with weight, convincing emotions and space constructed through perspective.

In a few words

Born near Florence, Masaccio transformed painting by giving figures volume, natural emotions and a place in a space constructed through perspective. He died at just 27, yet became one of the great pioneers of the Italian Renaissance.

01 / LOOK

The San Giovenale Triptych

1422 · Church of San Giovenale, Cascia di Reggello

Look closely
Look at the Virgin and Child, the angels, the saints at the sides and the way the figures occupy their space.
What it tells us
The bodies acquire weight and volume; light begins to give them a tangible presence.

02 / LOOK

The Trinity

c. 1425–1428 · Santa Maria Novella, Florence

Look closely
Look at the coffered vault, the receding lines, the kneeling donors and the skeleton below.
What it tells us
Perspective creates the illusion of a chapel opening into the wall. The skeleton reminds us of human mortality.

Source: Web Gallery of Art · Public domain

03 / LOOK

The Raising of the Son of Theophilus and Saint Peter Enthroned

c. 1425–1427, completed later · Brancacci Chapel, Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence

Look closely
Look at the boy brought back to life in the centre, Saint Peter enthroned on the right, the crowd and the buildings that organise the scene.
What it tells us
The figures inhabit a convincing urban space. Masaccio began this fresco and Filippino Lippi completed it: not every figure was painted by the same hand.

Source: Web Gallery of Art · Public domain

04 / LOOK

The Expulsion of Adam and Eve

c. 1426–1427 · Brancacci Chapel, Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence

Look closely
Look at Adam hiding his face, Eve crying out, their naked bodies in motion and their cast shadows.
What it tells us
Shame and grief become visible. The powerful bodies, modelled by light, give the sacred story a human intensity.

Saint Peter Healing the Sick with His Shadow

In a Florentine street, the façades create perspective and the sick occupy an everyday setting.

The essentials

Recognising a painting by Masaccio

He shows people with weight, emotion and gesture, within a coherent space constructed through perspective.

◈ Volume

The figures become solid and seem to occupy real space.

△ Perspective

Architecture and receding lines organise depth.

☀ Light

Shadows model the bodies and strengthen their presence.

♡ Humanity

Faces and gestures convey recognisable feelings.

Read these clues together: perspective, volume and light also appear in the work of other Renaissance artists.
