From Chloris to Flora: the birth of a flower goddess

Observe the transformation Why are flowers emerging from this young woman’s mouth?

On the right of Primavera , Botticelli makes the passage from Chloris to Flora visible. In his Fasti , the Roman poet Ovid tells the story of Chloris, a nymph — a deity associated with nature. One spring day Zephyr, the god of the west wind , sees her. She moves away; he pursues her. She flees, but he seizes her and forces himself upon her. Zephyr then marries her and gives her a garden of perpetual spring. He grants her power over flowers: Chloris becomes Flora, the Roman goddess of flowering . The ancient account presents their marriage as making amends for the initial violence.

Botticelli brings both moments into one image. On the right, Chloris still turns an anxious face towards Zephyr, flowers escaping from her mouth. Beside her, she appears as Flora, wearing a flowered dress and scattering roses. Two women follow one another, yet tell a single story. Beneath the abundance of flowers, the painting preserves the memory of violence before transformation.
