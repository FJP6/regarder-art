Thematic journey · 03

Exploring the Body and Beauty

Learn to see, dare to look.

From the anguish of Adam and Eve to the sensuality of Venus, Renaissance artists discovered countless ways of depicting the body. Six chapters invite you to examine gestures, muscles, beauty and desire — always returning to the artworks themselves.

← Back to journeys

01 · Explore

The Body Finds Its Emotions

From anguished faces to physical presence, the body now reveals what a human being feels.

Masaccio

The Expulsion of Adam and Eve from Paradise

Shame and grief are visible in faces and gestures. The body conveys emotion rather than merely illustrating a sacred story.

Public domain · fresco reproduction
Donatello

Penitent Mary Magdalene

Carved wood, an emaciated body and loose hair reveal a moving kind of beauty, far removed from the classical ideal.

Image and credit reused from the site’s Donatello page
Mantegna

The Dead Christ

The striking foreshortening brings the body close to the viewer. Look at the feet, hands and face.

Public domain

Look closer: Which gesture or expression makes this body feel human?

02 · Explore

Daring to Show the Nude

The nude is no longer simply an echo of Antiquity: it becomes vulnerable, alive and sometimes unsettling.

Masaccio

The Baptism of the Neophytes

The shivering young man makes the nude deeply human. Notice his hunched shoulders and tightly held arms.

Brancacci Chapel fresco, Florence · public-domain artwork reproduction
Donatello

Bronze David

A victorious yet vulnerable adolescent: his relaxed pose, smile and accessories challenge the conventional image of a hero.

Photo: Rufus46 · CC BY 3.0
Michelangelo

The Dying Slave

The body seems to surrender even as it twists. Marble conveys both muscular strength and release.

Photo: Frank Kovalchek · CC BY 2.0

Look closer: How does the pose change our understanding of the nude?

03 · Explore

Setting the Body in Motion

Bodies twist, struggle, run and rise. Artists explore the full range of their movement.

Antonio del Pollaiuolo

Battle of the Ten Nudes

Muscles become a language of movement. Compare bodies seen from the front, side and back.

Public domain · National Gallery of Canada / Google Arts & Culture
After Michelangelo

The Battle of Cascina

In this early copy of a lost composition, bodies turn, rise and intersect in a scene of collective commotion.

Copy by Bastiano da Sangallo, 1542 · public domain
Luca Signorelli

The Resurrection of the Flesh

Bodies emerge from the ground, stretch and stand: a remarkable exploration of the possibilities of the nude.

Public domain · Web Gallery of Art

Look closer: Where does each body lead your eye?

04 · Explore

Inventing an Ideal of Beauty

Proportion, harmony and mythology: how can ideal beauty be represented?

Botticelli

The Birth of Venus

The elongated body and flowing contours create a poetic ideal shaped more by line than by anatomical accuracy.

Public domain
Lorenzo di Credi

Venus

A standing Venus, almost motionless: her body is softly modelled in a restrained pose.

Public domain · Web Gallery of Art
Raphael

The Three Graces

Three figures, three viewpoints: the same ideal of beauty is revealed from the front, back and three-quarter view.

Public domain · Web Gallery of Art

Look closer: What distinguishes idealised beauty from a real body?

05 · Explore

When Beauty Becomes Desire

Nudity takes on different meanings through gazes, settings and gestures: from a dreamed-of goddess to a woman who looks directly at us.

Giorgione

Sleeping Venus

Her body echoes the curves of the landscape. Nudity becomes serene, visible to us yet never meeting our gaze.

Public domain · Google Art Project
Titian

Venus of Urbino

This time Venus looks directly at us. The domestic setting, her hand and the fabrics make the scene more intimate.

Public domain
Bronzino

An Allegory with Venus and Cupid

Smooth skin, ambiguous gestures and intersecting gazes turn beauty into a sensual and moral enigma.

Public domain · National Gallery, London

Look closer: Who is looking at whom, and how does that change the scene?

06 · Explore

The Body Becomes Superhuman

Anatomy becomes monumental, expressive, almost impossible: the body transcends human scale.

Michelangelo

David

The monumental body stands still, yet remains alert. Look at the hands, gaze and weight-bearing leg.

Photo: George M. Groutas · CC BY 2.0
Raphael

The Transfiguration

The composition contrasts Christ’s luminous, suspended body with the restless, expressive figures below.

Photo: Matthieu.lett.ube · CC BY 4.0
Giambologna

Hercules and the Centaur Nessus

The sculpture spirals through space: to grasp the struggle, imagine walking around the figures.

Photo: Frank Fleschner · CC BY 2.0

Look closer: How does the artist give the body extraordinary power?
