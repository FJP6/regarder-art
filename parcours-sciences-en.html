Art and science

Learn to see, dare to look.

When artists seek to understand the world: fifteen works reveal how geometry, invention, anatomy and observation shaped the Renaissance.

← Back to the five themes

01 · Measure — Geometry creates space

Painters do more than depict a scene: they construct a space that the eye can explore.

Learn to look — Follow the lines!

Perspective is something to see as well as understand. Show the guides on each work, then hide them to view the painting on its own.

1. Piero della Francesca

The edges of the paving slabs and architecture lead the eye towards the Flagellation. The red lines extend selected visible edges: their convergence helps explain how depth is constructed.

2. Melozzo da Forlì

Here, columns, pilasters and arches organise the scene. The blue lines highlight the architectural verticals without claiming to establish a single vanishing point.

3. Carlo Crivelli

The edges of the street, cornices and façades guide the eye into the distance. The red lines follow these visible directions, showing how architecture creates a corridor of depth.

Look closely: these teaching guides are indicative. They highlight directions rather than provide a scientific geometric reconstruction of the paintings.

02 · Invent — Drawing becomes a machine

Drawing helps artists think through a mechanism, reveal its components and imagine how it works.

03 · Understand — The human body, between science and beauty

Observing the body, its muscles and its proportions transforms the depiction of human beings.

04 · Observe — Nature under close scrutiny

Animals, plants and feathers become subjects worthy of close observation.

05 · Feel — Painting the invisible

Beyond accurate forms, artists seek to make emotions and thoughts visible.
